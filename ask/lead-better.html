How do I get more from my team?

Answered from 2 books and 1 failure case · simple English · Hindi and Hinglish in the reader

The short answer

The same team performs at 2x or 0.5x depending on the leader. Ask questions instead of giving answers, debate ideas hard, and hand over whole problems, not chores.

The books behind the answer

Multipliers

Liz Wiseman · 2010 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Multipliers amplify the genius around them; Diminishers shut it down. One leader gets 2x from a team; the other gets 50%.

  • The Multiplier Effect: 2x or 0.5x: Multipliers double output by harvesting the room's intelligence; Diminishers halve it by being the smartest person in it.
  • Ask Big Questions, Don't Give Big Answers: People feel smarter around leaders who ask, because they're the ones doing the thinking.
  • Give the Whole Problem, Not the Chore: Ownership is the ultimate multiplier. Hand over the entire outcome, with context and trust.

No Rules Rules

Reed Hastings & Erin Meyer · 2020 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Hire the best, tell them the truth, and let them make decisions - the Netflix culture machine, explained by its founder.

  • Context, Not Control: Spread strategy and reasoning so people can make aligned decisions themselves.

A failure that proves it

Why did General Electric fail?

2018 · BUSINESS · Loss: From $600B market cap to ~$100B and ejection from the Dow

GE was the world's most valuable company, then financial engineering replaced real leadership and the empire decayed.

Execution beats optics. If the core businesses can't carry the story, no amount of buybacks, restructuring or PowerPoint will. Keep it simple enough to manage honestly - complexity is where decay hides.
