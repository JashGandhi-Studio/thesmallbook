How do I become a better leader?

Answered from 5 books and 2 failure cases · simple English · Hindi and Hinglish in the reader

The short answer

Leadership is service with a bigger platform: protect your team, remove their obstacles, and own the failures publicly while giving away the credit. People follow those who carry weight, not those who collect titles. The best leaders are the ones who make themselves replaceable.

Extreme Ownership

Jocko Willink & Leif Babin · 2015 · 8 min · 6 lessons

There are no bad teams, only bad leaders. Own everything in your world - the blame, the fix, and therefore the power.

  • Extreme Ownership: The Mirror Is the Only Place to Look: When it fails, it's yours. When it succeeds, give the credit away. That's the whole job.

Simon Sinek · 2014 · 8 min · 6 lessons

In the Marines, officers eat after their men - and the men would die for them. Leadership is not rank; it's choosing to protect the people in your care.

  • The Circle of Safety: When people feel safe, they perform. Sacrifice first, and the tribe protects you back.

Great cultures aren't built on talent or perks. They run on three signals: you're safe here, we share weakness, and this is where we're going.

  • The Vulnerability Loop: Weakness Shared Is Trust Built: The best teams run on psychological safety. Leaders go first with honesty.

A failure that proves it

Why did Enron fail?

2001 · FRAUD · Loss: $74B shareholder value, 20,000 jobs

Enron hired the smartest people and rewarded the worst behavior. Culture eats strategy for breakfast.

Culture is what you tolerate - a workplace where bad news is career death guarantees you'll hear it last, when it's fatal. And never hold your salary AND savings in the same company's fate.

Why did General Electric fail?

2018 · BUSINESS · Loss: From $600B market cap to ~$100B and ejection from the Dow

GE was the world's most valuable company, then managed itself into a shadow. Leaders who stop leading the core, lose the crown.

Execution beats optics. If the core businesses can't carry the story, no amount of buybacks, restructuring or PowerPoint will. Keep it simple enough to manage honestly - complexity is where decay hides.
