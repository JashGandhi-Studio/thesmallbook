Thinking in Systems

2008 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Stop blaming people and events - behavior comes from structure. Learn to see stocks, flows, and feedback loops, and you'll find the real levers.

Donella Meadows - lead author of the world-modeling classic 'The Limits to Growth' - distilled a career of systems science into one humane insight: systems cause their own behavior. A system is elements + interconnections + a PURPOSE (revealed by what it does, not what it says), organized as STOCKS (accumulations you can measure: money in the bank, trust in a marriage, fish in the sea) changed by FLOWS (rates in and out), governed by FEEDBACK LOOPS - balancing loops that stabilize (thermostats, hunger) and...

Lessons in the summary

  • Stocks, Flows, and Why Change Feels So Slow
  • Feedback Loops: The Engines of Everything
  • System Traps: The Archetypes That Eat Organizations
  • Leverage Points: Where to Push a System
  • The System Is the Behavior: Fix the Structure, Not the People
  • The Power of Delays: Why Patient Systems Win

Lines worth keeping

  • A system is never the sum of its parts; it's the product of their interactions.
  • Purposes are deduced from behavior, not from rhetoric or stated goals.
  • We can't control systems or figure them out. But we can dance with them!
