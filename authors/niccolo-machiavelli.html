The Prince

1532 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Power has its own rules. Learn them - to lead, or to recognize those who play them on you.

Written in 1513 by a disgraced Florentine diplomat, The Prince is the first book to describe power as it is, not as it should be. Machiavelli's advice to rulers: it is safer to be feared than loved, appear virtuous while acting as circumstances demand, and adapt your style to the times. Five centuries later it remains the most quoted manual of political and corporate strategy - because it describes the game everyone is actually playing.

Lessons in the summary

  • Better to Be Feared Than Loved
  • The Fox and the Lion
  • Fortune Favors the Bold
  • Appearances Are the Prince
  • Win the People, Crush the Opposition
  • It Is Better to Be Feared Than Loved - If You Cannot Be Both

Lines worth keeping

  • It is much safer to be feared than loved, if one of the two has to be lacking.
  • Everyone sees what you appear to be, few experience what you really are.
  • Never do an enemy a small injury.
