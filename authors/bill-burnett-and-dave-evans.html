Designing Your Life

2016 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Two Stanford design professors apply design thinking to life: reframe the problem, prototype cheaply, and choose rather than decide.

Burnett and Evans argue that a life is a design problem, and design problems are solved by building and testing rather than by thinking until certainty arrives. Their method, reframe the unhelpful belief, accept that there are many possible lives, prototype the options, and use the process to pick, removes the pressure of the single right answer.

Lessons in the summary

  • Start Where You Are
  • Reframe the Broken Belief
  • Many Lives, Not One
  • Prototype
  • The Gravity Problem
  • Choose, Do Not Decide
  • Failure Is Data
  • Build the Life

Lines worth keeping

  • There is not one right life for you. There are many you could build.
  • Prototype the life before you commit to it.
  • Choosing is a skill, and it is different from deciding.
