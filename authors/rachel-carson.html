The Sense of Wonder

1965 · 7 min · 8 lessons

A child needs one adult who can share the wonder. Rachel Carson, who would later wake the world with Silent Spring, believed feeling comes before facts, and that once wonder is seeded, knowledge grows on its own for a lifetime.

Rachel Carson wrote this short book from exploring tide pools, woods and night skies with her young grandnephew Roger. Against the idea that children need nature facts, she argues they need companions in astonishment: adults willing to get wet, stay out past bedtime for owls, and receive a child's discoveries as news.
