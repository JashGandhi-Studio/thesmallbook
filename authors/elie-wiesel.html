Night

1960 · 8 min · 8 lessons

At fifteen, Elie Wiesel was deported to Auschwitz with his family. Night is his account of losing his father, his faith and his childhood, and his vow that the world would not be allowed to forget.

Elie Wiesel's memoir is the essential first-person testimony of the Holocaust: the cattle cars, the selection, the crematoria, the death march, and the slow destruction of a father-son bond by starvation. Its power is its restraint, short declarative sentences carrying unbearable weight. Elie Wiesel wrote it to bear witness against forgetting and indifference, which he called the true enemies, more dangerous even than hatred.
