The Undercover Economist

2005 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Tim Harford uses coffee, supermarkets and traffic to explain scarcity, information and unintended consequences.

The book's organising question is scarcity: who gets the thing that is limited, and by what mechanism. Harford shows that prices ration, that information is unevenly held, and that intervening without understanding the system usually produces a different result from the one intended. The examples are ordinary, which is the point, and the thinking is transferable.

Lines worth keeping

  • Scarcity means something has to ration. The only question is what.
  • When someone knows more than you do, you are the product being traded.
  • Every intervention produces a second effect you did not intend.
