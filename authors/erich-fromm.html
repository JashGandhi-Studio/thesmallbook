The Art of Loving

1956 · 8 min · 8 lessons

Most people think the problem is finding the right person to love. Fromm says the problem is learning how to love at all.

writing in 1956, Erich Fromm argued that love is treated like a market transaction, two people trading qualities, when it is actually a capacity that must be developed like music or carpentry. Mature love has four elements: care, responsibility, respect and knowledge. It preserves the integrity of both people instead of dissolving them into each other, and it begins with giving rather than receiving.

Lessons in the summary

  • Love Is an Art, Not Luck
  • Care, Responsibility, Respect, Knowledge
  • Giving Is Not Losing
  • Self-Love Is Not Selfishness
  • Love in a Market Society
  • Motherly and Fatherly Love Are Two Schools
  • Love of Strangers Is the Test of the Skill
  • Concentration, Patience, and Supreme Concern

Lines worth keeping

  • Love is an activity, not a passive affect.
  • Immature love says: I love you because I need you. Mature love says: I need you because I love you.
  • To love somebody is not just a strong feeling. It is a decision, it is a judgment, it is a promise.
