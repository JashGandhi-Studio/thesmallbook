Algorithms to Live By

2016 · 11 min · 8 lessons

The best algorithms for sorting, searching, scheduling and stopping are also instructions for how to live.

Brian Christian and Tom Griffiths noticed that the problems computer scientists have formalised, optimal stopping, explore versus exploit, caching, scheduling, overfitting, are the same problems humans face daily with less clarity. The result is not a metaphor. It is usable mathematics: when to stop looking, when to commit, what to keep and what to throw away, and how to know when more information stops helping.

Lessons in the summary

  • Know When to Stop Looking
  • Explore, Then Exploit
  • Sort Your Life by What Arrives
  • Keep What You Use
  • Schedule the Shortest Thing First
  • Do Not Overfit Your Life
  • Relax and Let Queues Form
  • Compute Forgiveness

Lines worth keeping

  • The secretary problem gives you a number: look at the first thirty seven percent, then commit.
  • Every second spent comparing has a cost, and ignoring it is how people stay paralysed.
  • A good rule is one you can follow when you are tired and wrong.
