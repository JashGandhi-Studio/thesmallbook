The WEIRDest People in the World

2020 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Joseph Henrich argues that the WEIRD mind is a product of a specific history, especially the medieval church's marriage rules, and that it is not the human default.

Psychology's standard subject is a WEIRD undergraduate, and Henrich shows how unrepresentative that person is on fairness, conformity, patience and trust. The explanation he builds is institutional: the Catholic church's ban on kin marriage broke extended family structures across Europe, and a cascade followed, impersonal markets, voluntary associations, literacy, a self that thinks of itself as an individual. Culture and institutions shape minds, not the other way round.
