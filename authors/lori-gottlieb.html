Maybe You Should Talk to Someone

2019 · 11 min · 10 lessons

A therapist loses her boyfriend, her identity and her footing, and goes to therapy herself. Behind the wall of the consulting room, five patients (and one therapist) show how change actually happens: slowly, honestly, and never the way we planned.

Gottlieb interleaves her own therapy with the cases in her practice: a Hollywood producer who insists everyone else is the problem, a newlywed with a terminal diagnosis choosing how to live her last years, a twenty-something who has swapped one alcohol-soaked evening for another, a seventy-nine-year-old who insists her life is over before it began. The insights cut across them all: we are unreliable narrators of our own stories, the presenting problem is rarely the real one, change means grieving the fantasy life...

  • Joy in the Small Hours
  • Everyone Is Everyone Else, and Us

Lines worth keeping

  • Almost every struggle is rooted in a loss or fear of loss.
  • There's a difference between pain and suffering: pain is what happens to us; suffering is what we do with it.
  • People often say they want to change, but what they really want is to have already changed.
