The Course of Love

2016 · 8 min · 8 lessons

Romanticism promised that the right person makes love effortless. Reality: love is a skill of interpreting each other's madness with generosity.

Alain de Botton follows Rabih and Kirsten from courtship through decades of marriage, pausing the novel to comment like a kind therapist. His target is the Romantic script: find the one, feel understood without speaking, and live exempt from loneliness and conflict. Against it he proposes a classical view: partners are inevitably difficult, love is the patient work of educating each other, and sulking, blame and coldness are failed attempts to teach what we cannot yet say.

Lessons in the summary

  • The Romantic Script Sets You Up to Fail
  • Sulking Is a Lesson That Cannot Get Said
  • Partners Educate Each Other or Irritate Each Other
  • Love Needs Translation Because Partners Are Foreign
  • Anxiety and Avoidance Dance Together
  • Children Teach a Couple What Love Costs
  • The Maturity of Being Ready to Learn
  • Ready Rather Than Right: The Book's Final Exam

Lines worth keeping

  • Love is a skill, not just an enthusiasm.
  • We are all, in private, idiots. Marriage is a hopeful, generous, infinitely kind drama of gradual self-improvement.
  • The person who is truly best suited to us is not the person who shares our tastes, but the person who can negotiate differences with intelligence and grace.
