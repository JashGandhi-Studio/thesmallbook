12 Rules for Life

2018 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Life is suffering; the antidote is responsibility. Fix yourself first, aim at meaning, and tell the truth - or at least don't lie.

Peterson - clinical psychologist and professor - frames existence as the eternal navigation between chaos (the unknown, potential, catastrophe) and order (structure, tradition, tyranny when excessive), with meaning found on the border. His twelve rules are practical philosophy welded from psychology, neuroscience, mythology, and clinical practice: posture changes neurochemistry (Rule 1's lobsters), you deserve your own care (Rule 2), friendships should lift (Rule 3), compare yourself to yesterday's you (Rule 4),...

Lessons in the summary

  • Stand Up Straight: The Lobster's Lesson
  • Treat Yourself Like Someone You're Responsible for Helping
  • Yesterday's You Is the Only Fair Rival
  • Clean Your Room Before You Criticize the World
  • Meaning Over Expedience, Truth Over Comfort
  • Compare Yourself to Who You Were Yesterday, Not to Who Someone Else Is Today

Lines worth keeping

  • Compare yourself to who you were yesterday, not to who someone else is today.
  • Set your house in perfect order before you criticize the world.
  • Pursue what is meaningful, not what is expedient.
