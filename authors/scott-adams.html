How to Fail at Almost Everything and Still Win Big

2013 · 8 min · 6 lessons

The Dilbert creator failed at everything - then won by using systems instead of goals, stacking mediocre skills into rarity, and managing energy like capital.

Adams' resume before Dilbert is a graveyard: failed restaurants, failed inventions, failed video game, a corporate career capped by being told he'd never be promoted - and the book's engine is what the failures taught: GOALS ARE FOR LOSERS, SYSTEMS ARE FOR WINNERS (a goal - 'lose 10kg' - keeps you in pre-success failure-state daily and expires on achievement; a system - 'learn to eat right, be active daily' - pays every time you use it and never expires; wannabe screenwriters have a goal, working writers have a...

Lessons in the summary

  • Systems Beat Goals: Design for Daily Wins
  • The Talent Stack: Get Good, Not Great, at Compatible Things
  • Energy, Luck Surface, and Failing Forward
  • The Moist Robot: Diet, Exercise, and Programming Your Own Hardware
  • The Happiness Formula: Set Lower Expectations, Raise Your Baseline
  • Simplify, Then Simplify Again: The Complexity Tax

Lines worth keeping

  • Losers have goals. Winners have systems.
  • Every skill you acquire doubles your odds of success.
  • Failure is where success likes to hide in plain sight.
