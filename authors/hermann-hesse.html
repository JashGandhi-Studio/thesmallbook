Siddhartha

1922 · 8 min · 10 lessons

A Brahmin's son tries asceticism, teachers, wealth and pleasure, and finds wisdom in none of them, until a river teaches him what no teacher could: truth cannot be given, only lived.

Hesse's small novel has outlived a century of self-help because it argues the opposite of most of them: no teaching, however perfect, can transmit wisdom. Siddhartha studies under ascetics, sits at the feet of the Buddha himself, then walks away, because even the Buddha's truth is secondhand. He loses himself in money and desire, hits bottom, and learns to listen, to the river, which flows everywhere at once, holding every stage of life in the same water.
