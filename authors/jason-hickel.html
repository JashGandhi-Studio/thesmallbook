The Divide

2017 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Jason Hickel argues that global inequality was produced by centuries of extraction, and that the standard story of growth is a narration of the winners.

Hickel's case is historical and accounting based. He tracks how the poor world's wealth moved to the rich world through colonialism, unequal exchange, debt and structural adjustment, and argues that the famous development curve is drawn with a line that only fits one side of the world. Whether or not you accept every number, the challenge is serious: if the divergence was made, it can be unmade.

Lines worth keeping

  • Poverty is not an original condition. It is a relationship.
  • Colonialism did not end. It changed its instruments.
  • The story of the great divergence is told from one end of the world.
