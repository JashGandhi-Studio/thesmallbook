Superhuman: Life at the Extremes of Our Capacity

2018 · 8 min · 8 lessons

Free divers who hold their breath for eleven minutes, memory champions, ultra runners, super-recognisers: Rowan Hooper spent years with people at the far edges of human capacity, asking what is nature, what is nurture, and what is available to the rest of us.

Journalist and biologist Rowan Hooper studies extreme humans, people who run 100 miles, memorise decks of cards, survive impossible odds, stay serene under fire, and asks the only useful question: how much of that is us? Each chapter pairs a trait, endurance, memory, resilience, intelligence, happiness, with the science of its limits, finding the same pattern everywhere: genetics deals a range, and training, mindset and environment decide where inside the range a life lands.

Lessons in the summary

  • The Range Is Genetic; Where You Land Is Not
  • Resilience Is a Skill With a Physiology
  • Happiness Has Extremes Too, and They Are Ordinary
  • Endurance Is Mostly a Negotiation With the Brain
  • Intelligence Is a Choir, Not a Solo
  • Sleep, Focus and the Quiet Extremes
  • Trainability Is the Trait Under All the Traits
  • The Ethics of Knowing Your Extremes

Lines worth keeping

  • Extreme people show us what the human operating system can do when pushed.
  • Genes load the gun, but the environment pulls most of the triggers.
  • Almost every capacity we admire turns out to be more trainable than we were told.
