Prisoners of Geography

2015 · 10 min · 10 lessons

World politics makes sense only on a physical map: mountains, rivers, plains and ports decide why Russia wants Ukraine, why China needs the sea, why Africa struggles to connect, and why no leader, however clever, escapes geography.

Marshall tours ten regions and shows the terrain under the headlines. Russia's fear of invasion across the North European Plain drives Ukraine; China's core is the fertile east and its navy is bottled by the first island chain; the US is the geographically blessed superpower (two oceans, farmland, rivers); Africa's rivers and coastline frustrate trade; the Middle East's rivers and oil make it eternally contested. The argument is not determinism but constraint: leaders are prisoners of geography, forced choices...
