The E-Myth Revisited

1995 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Great technicians build themselves jobs, not businesses. Build systems that run the business, so the business doesn't run you.

The Entrepreneurial Myth: that businesses are started by entrepreneurs. Reality: they're started by TECHNICIANS having an 'entrepreneurial seizure' - the baker who assumes baking skill means bakery-running skill, the 'fatal assumption' that technical work and a business doing that work are the same thing. Result: the technician buys a job, becomes the business's most overworked employee, and burns out. Gerber's fix: balance three internal personalities (Entrepreneur/vision, Manager/order, Technician/craft),...

  • The Fatal Assumption & the Three-Personality War
  • The Franchise Prototype: McDonald's as a Thinking Tool
  • Work ON It: The Business Development Process
  • Your Primary Aim: The Business Serves the Life
  • The Turnkey Revolution: Systems Over Heroes
  • Management by Exception: Run the Business, Don't Be It

Lines worth keeping

  • If your business depends on you, you don't own a business - you have a job.
  • The problem is not your business; the problem is you.
  • Organize around business functions, not personalities.
