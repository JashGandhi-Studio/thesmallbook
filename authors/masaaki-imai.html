Kaizen

1986 · 7 min · 9 lessons

KAIZEN = change for the better - not big jumps, but endless small improvements that compound.

Imai's book introduced the West to the idea that turned Japanese manufacturing into a global force: improvement is not a special event by experts, but a daily discipline by everyone. Small standardised improvements, measured relentlessly, outrun occasional big innovations. The three pillars: eliminate waste (muda), standardise the best-known way, and treat problems as treasures - a problem now visible is progress.

Lessons in the summary

  • Small Steps Beat Big Leaps
  • Waste Is the Enemy
  • Problems Are Treasures
  • Standardise, Then Improve
  • Gemba: Go and See
  • Standardization: The Unsexy Engine
  • The Kata: Daily Practice as the System
  • The Second Question: Why, Then Why, Then Fix
  • The Culture of the Kaizen Mind

Lines worth keeping

  • KAIZEN means improvement. Moreover, it means continuing improvement in personal life, home life, social life, and working life.
  • When improvement is standardised, it becomes the new baseline for the next improvement.
  • To go to work day after day and do the same thing the same way, without improvement, is not management.
