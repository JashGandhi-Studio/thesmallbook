Doughnut Economics

2017 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Kate Raworth redraws economics as a doughnut: a social foundation below which nobody should fall, and an ecological ceiling above which no economy can survive.

Raworth argues that economics is a design discipline and the twentieth century design is failing. Instead of an endless growth curve, she offers a doughnut: enough for everyone to live well, within planetary limits. The seven moves she proposes, changing the goal, the picture, the nature, the systems, the distribution, the design and the practice, are a rebuild, not a repair.

Lessons in the summary

  • Change the Goal
  • See the Whole Picture
  • Put the Planet Back In
  • Think in Systems
  • Share the Value
  • Design to Distribute
  • Do Not Grow Up, Grow Better
  • Be a Practitioner

Lines worth keeping

  • An economy should meet human needs within the means of the planet.
  • Growth is a means. Getting the goal wrong is the central error of the discipline.
  • If the rules are a design, they can be redesigned.
