The Friction Project

2024 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Sutton and Rao separate the friction that wastes people's time from the friction that prevents careless decisions.

Friction is anything that slows people down, and the authors argue that the right goal is not frictionless operation but selective friction: remove the pointless obstacles that stop people doing their jobs, and add deliberate difficulty to the decisions that are too easy to make badly. The distinction between the two is the whole discipline.

  • Friction in Decisions

  • Sustaining It

Lines worth keeping

  • Friction is not always waste. Some of it is your only defence against a bad decision.
  • The people who know where the friction is are not the people who designed the process.
  • Adding a step to a decision can be a gift. Adding a step to a task is usually theft.
