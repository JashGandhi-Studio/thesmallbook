Guns, Germs, and Steel

1997 · 12 min · 10 lessons

Why did Eurasians conquer the Americas and not the reverse? Not biology or genius: the accident of geography gave some peoples domesticable plants and animals first, and with them, germs, technology and immunities that decided everything.

Diamond answers Yali's question (why did white men build so much cargo?) with a chain of causes rooted in geography: the Fertile Crescent's edible plants and large domesticable mammals enabled farming; farming fed specialists, states and, crucially, proximity to herd animals, whose diseases became Eurasians' deadliest weapon. East-west continental axes spread crops and technology quickly; the Americas' and Africa's north-south axes stalled them. Steel, writing, political organization and naval technology followed...

Lines worth keeping

  • History followed different courses for different peoples because of differences among peoples' environments, not because of biological differences between peoples themselves.
  • Much of human history has consisted of unequal conflicts between the haves and the have-nots.
  • The spread of human populations... followed the axes of the continents.
