Made in Japan: Akio Morita and Sony

1986 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Akio Morita's memoir of building Sony from a bombed-out Tokyo workshop: the tape recorder nobody bought, the transistor license nobody wanted, and the Walkman nobody researched.

Morita (with engineer-partner Masaru Ibuka) founded Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo in 1946 in a bombed-out department store, failed publicly (an automatic rice cooker that burned rice), succeeded with Japan's first tape recorder (sold first to courts, which needed to record proceedings), licensed the transistor from Bell Labs (1953) when everyone else saw only hearing aids, built the world's first pocket transistor radio, fought the 'Made in Japan' junk stigma by renaming the company Sony (from sonus, sound), and in 1979...

Lessons in the summary

  • Lead the Public; Don't Ask Them What's Possible
  • Failure at the Scale of a Rice Cooker Is Tuition
  • A License Can Be the Whole Company
  • Name the Stigma, Then Kill It
  • Sell to the Customer Who Needs It Most First
  • Consensus Has a Speed Limit
  • Design Is a Moral Position
  • Superior Tech Loses to Superior Systems

Lines worth keeping

  • Our plan is to lead the public with new products rather than ask them what kind of products they want.
  • The public does not know what is possible. We do.
  • Careful monitoring of the thousand small things is what quality actually is.
