Nonviolent Communication

2003 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Behind every criticism is an unmet need trying to speak. Learn the OFNR grammar - observe, feel, need, request - and watch arguments dissolve.

Rosenberg - a psychologist who mediated everywhere from riot-torn schools to war zones - diagnosed everyday speech as quietly violent: moralistic judgments ('you're so selfish'), comparisons, denial of responsibility ('you make me angry'), and demands all trigger defense instead of connection. His alternative grammar, NVC, has four steps: OBSERVATION (state what a camera would record, without evaluation - 'you've arrived after 9 three times this week,' not 'you're always late'; mixing observation with judgment...

Lessons in the summary

  • Separate Observation From Evaluation
  • Feelings Are Gauges; Needs Are the Engine
  • Requests, Not Demands - and Empathy as First Aid
  • Self-Compassion in NVC: Mourning, Not Moralizing
  • The Four Components: Observation, Feeling, Need, Request
  • The Need Behind the Anger: Every Emotion Is a Need's Messenger

Lines worth keeping

  • Every criticism, judgment, diagnosis and expression of anger is the tragic expression of an unmet need.
  • Observing without evaluating is the highest form of intelligence.
  • When we hear the other person's feelings and needs, we recognize our common humanity.
