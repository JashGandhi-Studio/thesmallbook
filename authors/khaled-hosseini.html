The Kite Runner

2003 · 9 min · 8 lessons

Amir wins the kite tournament and betrays the friend who ran for him. Twenty years later, a phone call offers the sentence the novel is built around: there is a way to be good again.

Khaled Hosseini's novel follows Amir, a privileged Pashtun boy, and Hassan, his Hazara servant and devoted friend, from Kabul's kite-fighting winters through Soviet invasion, exile in California, and a return under the Taliban. It is a story about cowardice and its long interest payments, about fathers and sons, and about redemption as something done with the body and the years, not felt in the heart alone.
