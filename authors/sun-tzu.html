The Art of War

-500 · 8 min · 6 lessons

Every battle is won before it is fought: know yourself, know the enemy, choose the ground, and win - ideally without fighting at all.

Written for warring Chinese states ~500 BC and studied ever since by generals, CEOs, and negotiators, Sun Tzu's thirteen chapters form the foundational text of strategic thought. Its revolutionary core: war (and by extension all conflict - business, negotiation, competition) is won by CALCULATION, DECEPTION, and POSITIONING before contact, not by courage during it. The pillars: supreme excellence is breaking resistance WITHOUT fighting (the best victory leaves nothing destroyed); all warfare is based on deception...

Lessons in the summary

  • Win Without Fighting
  • All Warfare Is Based on Deception
  • Know Yourself, Know the Enemy
  • Win First, Then Fight: Position, Energy & Timing
  • The Nine Grounds: Read the Terrain Before You Fight
  • Spies and Intelligence: The Army That Knows Wins

Lines worth keeping

  • The supreme art of war is to subdue the enemy without fighting.
  • If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles.
  • Victorious warriors win first and then go to war, while defeated warriors go to war first and then seek to win.
