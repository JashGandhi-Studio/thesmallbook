Entangled Life

2020 · 11 min · 8 lessons

Merlin Sheldrake shows how fungi shape soils, forests, bodies and minds, and how little of it we have understood.

Fungi are everywhere, invisible, and structurally weird: neither plant nor animal, with bodies made of networks that behave more like membranes than organs. They decompose, exchange nutrients, partner with almost every plant, and live in our guts. Sheldrake's argument is about entanglement: the story of an individual organism is a story that cannot be told alone.

Lines worth keeping

  • Fungi are not plants. They are closer to animals.
  • A tree is not an organism so much as a point where several organisms meet.
  • Life is not a set of individuals competing. It is a web of relationships.
