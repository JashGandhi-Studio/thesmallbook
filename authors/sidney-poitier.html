The Measure of a Man

2000 · 8 min · 8 lessons

A boy from a Bahamian tomato farm, who arrived in New York with nothing and could barely read, became the actor who changed what a leading man could be. Poitier measures that life not in fame but in character.

Sidney Poitier's memoir traces his path from Cat Island poverty through dishwashing in New York, a lucky break at the American Negro Theatre, and a career of deliberate choices: refusing degrading roles when refusal meant hunger, and building a screen presence that carried dignity into America's living rooms. The measure of the title is his parents' standard, character over circumstance, by which he audits his successes, his failures as a father, and his survival of an industry that wanted a symbol and got a man.
