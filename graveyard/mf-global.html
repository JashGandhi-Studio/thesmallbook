Why Did MF Global Fail?

The Broker That Bet Its Clients' Money on European Debt

2011YEAR
MONEYCAUSE
$1.6 billion of customer funds went missing; the eighth-largest US bankruptcy; a former governor and Goldman chief at the helmTHE BURN

What happened

Jon Corzine's MF Global placed a $6.25 billion bet on European sovereign debt - bought cheap, hedged thin, maturing before the panic could last. The panic lasted. Downgrades and margin calls drained the firm in a week, and in the scramble, $1.6 billion of customer segregation money had leaked into the house's own hole - a cardinal sin in brokerage. Customers waited months; Corzine paid $100 million toward restitution and accepted a ban from running a futures dealer.

The fatal mistake

Using a brokerage's balance sheet for a directional macro bet while the segregation walls between house and client money quietly dissolved under funding stress.

The lesson

In any firm that holds your money, the question is not returns but segregation: where, in whose name, reconciled how often. The first law of brokerage is 'it's not the firm's money' - the second law is to count.
