Why Did The Dutch East India Company Fail?

The Richest Company in History Died of Its Own Dividends

1799YEAR
HISTORYCAUSE
Nearly 200 years of monopoly, a fleet of thousands, and a valuation estimated near $8 trillion in modern money - dissolved to nothingTHE BURN

What happened

The VOC was the first company with tradeable shares and paid extraordinary dividends for a century; its market cap would rank near the largest companies ever. But it stopped reinvesting: ships aged, profits left as dividends, corruption consumed the colonies, and when the Fourth Anglo-Dutch War destroyed the fleet in 1780-84, the company that had financed a golden age was a shell owing money to its own officers. Nationalised in 1796, formally dissolved in 1799.

The fatal mistake

Paying out the golden decades instead of compounding them - dividends and patronage consumed the fleet, the forts and the trading posts that produced them.

The lesson

A moat is a depreciating asset that must be refilled with earnings. Companies that distribute their edge eventually meet a war they cannot afford.
