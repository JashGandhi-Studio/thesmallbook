Why Did The Holocaust: Bureaucracy in the Service of Extermination Fail?

The Machinery of Murder, Administered

1945YEAR
HISTORYCAUSE
Six million Jews and millions more murdered by a modern stateTHE BURN

What happened

The Holocaust was the state sponsored, bureaucratically organised persecution and murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators between 1941 and 1945, alongside the murder of millions of others: Roma, disabled people, Polish and Soviet civilians, Soviet prisoners of war, political opponents, homosexual men and others the regime marked for destruction. It proceeded in documented stages: legal exclusion beginning in 1933, the Nuremberg Laws redefining citizenship by race, organised violence in 1938, ghettoisation after invasion, mass shootings by mobile killing units that murdered over a million people in open air massacres, and finally the extermination camps, Auschwitz Birkenau, Treblinka, Belzec, Sobibor, Chelmno and Majdanek, where most deportees were killed on arrival. What distinguishes it in this graveyard of failures is that it was an achievement of administration: census data, rail timetables, chemical contracts, personnel files. Ordinary institutions performed each small function, and the sum was genocide. This library includes it because its lessons are load bearing for every other page here: about categories applied to people, about bystanders, about the duty of memory that our books on Viktor E. Frankl and Elie Wiesel carry personally.

The fatal mistake

To list mistakes risks obscenity; the perpetrators largely achieved their aims. The failures belong to the world around them and to the institutions inside Germany that surrendered in sequence: courts that applied racial law as ordinary jurisprudence, universities that expelled and complied, churches largely silent, businesses that bid for contracts, and foreign governments that held refugee conferences and closed their doors. Within Germany, each profession found the small accommodation that let the machine proceed, which is precisely how the machine proceeded. The catastrophe's enabling condition was not universal sadism. It was universal careerism meeting a categorised minority, plus the bystander's calculation that someone else's rights were not worth his pension.

The lesson

Categories imposed on human beings by the state are weapons in storage; guard who may build them and what may be done with the lists. Institutions do not resist by culture or charter; they resist by individuals refusing at cost, early, when refusal is still cheap and therefore rare. Bystanding is a decision with beneficiaries. And memory is infrastructure: the testimony of survivors, the archives, the trials, exist because forgetting is the perpetrators' last weapon, and every generation is re-recruited to disarm it. Never again is not a sentiment. It is a maintenance schedule.
