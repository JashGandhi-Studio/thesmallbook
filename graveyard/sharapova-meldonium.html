Why Did Maria Sharapova Fail?

A Diabetes Drug, a Missed Email, and $200 Million of Endorsements

2016YEAR
FAMECAUSE
A 15-month ban at 29; sponsors suspended or dropped within 48 hoursTHE BURN

What happened

Sharapova had taken meldonium for a decade for health issues; when it joined the banned list on 1 January 2016, she kept taking it and tested positive at the Australian Open. She announced it herself in March, sponsors fled in days - Nike suspended, TAG Heuer and Porsche paused - and the ban stretched to 15 months. She returned in 2017 to respect but not to the same endorsement empire: the clean-sport brands never fully came back.

The fatal mistake

Delegating compliance to an inbox - a professional athlete's team treated a rule-change email as noise, and the liability landed entirely on the name above the logo.

The lesson

Personal brands are concentrated equity: one compliance failure deleverages everything at once. For any regulated career, the rules list is a daily document, not a forwarded mail.
