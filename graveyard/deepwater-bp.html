Why Did Deepwater Horizon Fail?

Saving Hours on a Safety Test Cost $65 Billion

2010YEAR
☠️ BUSINESSCAUSE
💸 $65B+, 11 lives, an oceanTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

BP's Macondo well was over budget and 43 days late — every extra day cost $1M. So decisions tilted: fewer centralizers than the design called for, a cement test skipped, ambiguous pressure-test results explained away, warnings from rig workers overridden. Each shortcut saved hours. Together they removed every barrier between the crew and the blowout that killed 11 people and poured 4 million barrels of oil into the Gulf for 87 straight days on live camera. Total cost to BP: over $65 billion — 65,000 times the daily overrun they were racing to avoid.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Letting schedule pressure reprice safety margins one 'reasonable' shortcut at a time — no single decision was insane; the SUM was catastrophic.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

Margins of safety look like waste right up until they're the only thing that mattered. When you're behind schedule, that's precisely when your standards are worth the most — pressure is the test, not the excuse.
