Why Did Carillion Fail?

The Government Contractor That Was Three Big Projects From Fine

2018YEAR
BUSINESSCAUSE
£1.5 billion of debt, a £900 million pension deficit, 43,000 jobs at risk; liquidated with no buyerTHE BURN

What happened

Carillion built hospitals, maintained prisons and ran school lunches across Britain - a pillar of public services that looked unbreakable. Inside, contracts were booked on aggressive revenue recognition, cash was managed to hit dividends, and loss-makers were 'corrected' by optimism. Three UK contracts (including the Royal Liverpool Hospital with its fault-riddled build) detonated the accounts. In January 2018 the company entered compulsory liquidation; MPs called it 'a story of recklessness, hubris and greed'.

The fatal mistake

Recognising imagined profits years ahead on contracts that were already losing - the accounting showed a going concern that the sites knew was a going collapse.

The lesson

Percentage-of-completion accounting is where hope becomes revenue. Track cash collected, not profit booked; on long contracts, the difference is the whole truth.
