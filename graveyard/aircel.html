Why Did Aircel Fail?

The Telco That Borrowed for Spectrum and Died Under the Debt

2018YEAR
☠️ BUSINESSCAUSE
💸 ₹40,000+ Cr debt; 100 million subscribers stranded; 15,000+ jobs lostTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

Aircel was once one of India's most promising telecoms — launched in 1999, it grew to 100 million subscribers and was a serious player in the pre-Jio era. Its fatal move came in 2010: borrowing heavily to buy 3G spectrum, betting that revenues would grow fast enough to service the debt. The bet didn't survive contact with reality — tariffs crashed, competition intensified, and then Reliance Jio's 2016 entry collapsed data prices to near zero. Aircel's debt had ballooned to over ₹40,000 crore while its revenues shrank. In 2018, after failed merger attempts, the company filed for bankruptcy — 100 million subscribers were left scrambling, and one of India's oldest private telcos simply ceased to exist.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Buying tomorrow's spectrum with today's borrowed money — the spectrum was a bet on future tariffs, and when tariffs collapsed, the bet was the company.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

In capital-heavy industries, the winners aren't the ones who buy the most future — they're the ones who survive until the future arrives. Aircel's lesson: leverage purchased on expected revenues is a short position on reality. If your break-even depends on prices staying high, someone will always find a way to make them low.
