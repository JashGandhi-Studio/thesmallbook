Why Did Quiznos Fail?

How to Franchise a Sandwich and Tax the Bread

2014YEAR
BUSINESSCAUSE
From about 4,900 US locations to a 2014 bankruptcy with a fifth of that, then hundredsTHE BURN

What happened

Quiznos toasted its way to nearly 5,000 US shops, then squeezed the wrong end of the sandwich: franchisees had to buy food through the company's approved suppliers at prices that ate the margin on every $5 sub, while the company stacked fees and securitised debt on top. Stores died faster than they opened; franchisee lawsuits multiplied; the 2014 bankruptcy cut the debt but not the math. Subway, which let its shops keep their margin, ate the market.

The fatal mistake

The franchisor's profit centre moved from growing brand value to taxing franchisee inputs - every store opening became a margin transfer instead of a compounding asset.

The lesson

In franchising, the unit's profit IS the product. When HQ makes money from what it sells to operators rather than what operators sell to customers, collapse is scheduled.

The antidote book

Profit FirstMike Michalowicz · Money & Finance · 6 lessons

Sales − Profit = Expenses. Take profit FIRST, hide it, and let the business survive on what remains - because it will.
