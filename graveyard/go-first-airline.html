Why Did Go First Airlines Fail?

Grounded by Engine Supply Chain Failures

2023YEAR
☠️ BUSINESSCAUSE
💸 6,500 crore INR bank exposure; insolvency liquidationTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

Go First (formerly GoAir), owned by the historic Wadia Group, operated as one of India's most disciplined budget carriers for nearly two decades, capturing 10 percent of domestic aviation with an all-Airbus A320 fleet. But the airline bet its entire modernization on Pratt & Whitney GTF engines. Over 2019 to 2022, unserviceable engine failures grounded half of Go First's 54-aircraft fleet due to supply chain shortages of replacement parts. With half its planes sitting idle on airport tarmacs while lease rents and staff salaries continued, cash reserves vanished. In May 2023, Go First voluntarily filed for insolvency, leaving Indian banks with over 6,500 crore INR in unpaid debt.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Relying on a single unproven engine supplier across the entire fleet without contingency lease provisions.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

Single-supplier dependencies in high-fixed-cost capital industries are existential risks. If one component manufacturer fails to deliver replacement parts, your entire fleet becomes grounded dead weight.
