Why Did Ofo Fail?

Ten Million Yellow Bikes and Nowhere to Park Them

2019YEAR
STARTUPCAUSE
Over $2 billion raised and burned; millions of users' deposits stranded; 'bike graveyards' became the decade's defining photoTHE BURN

What happened

Ofo invented dockless bike-sharing and scaled it like a blitzkrieg: tens of millions of bikes across twenty-plus countries, $1 deposits in the billions, bikes free or near-free to ride. Nobody's ride covered the bike's own cost, vandalism was industrial, and cities began impounding fleets by the ton. By 2019 Ofo had retreated from nearly every market; users queued months for deposit refunds while aerial photos of yellow bike mountains told the story.

The fatal mistake

Growth as the business model - acquisition economics measured rides, and every ride lost money on a depreciating, vandalised asset that cities eventually confiscated.

The lesson

Hardware networks need unit economics that survive contact with reality: theft, weather and city hall. Subsidised rides are not a moat; they are a countdown.
