Why Did Argentina's Corralito Fail?

The Week Argentina Had Five Presidents

2001YEAR
HISTORYCAUSE
The largest sovereign default in history at the time ($95-100B); deposits frozen; the peso's one-to-one peg shattered within weeksTHE BURN

What happened

After years of pegging the peso rigidly to the dollar and borrowing in dollars, Argentina lost market access in 2001. To stop the run, the government froze bank deposits - the 'corralito' - capping withdrawals and converting dollar accounts into devalued pesos. Riots followed; President De la Rúa fled the Casa Rosada by helicopter, and Argentina burned through five presidents in under two weeks. The default that followed was the largest the world had seen.

The fatal mistake

Defending an untetherable peg with borrowed dollars while promising savers convertibility - the promise was credible only until it wasn't, and then everything happened at once.

The lesson

Fixed exchange rates are political promises with expiry dates. When capital flight begins, the speed of adjustment decides who gets their money: usually, not the savers.
