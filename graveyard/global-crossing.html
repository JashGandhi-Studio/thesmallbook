Why Did Global Crossing Fail?

The Fiber Empire That Bankrupted Itself Connecting the World

2002YEAR
MONEYCAUSE
A $12.5 billion bankruptcy - the fourth largest in US history at the time; founder cashed out about $700 millionTHE BURN

What happened

Gary Winnick raised billions to carpet the ocean floor with fiber optics, betting the internet would need every strand. Traffic grew, but prices collapsed faster than capacity filled, and to keep revenue looking strong the company engaged in capacity-swap accounting that booked round-trip deals as sales. By January 2002 the fourth-biggest bankruptcy in American history had arrived; employees' retirements were stock, and Winnick had sold years earlier.

The fatal mistake

Treating capital-raising as the business model - the network was real, the demand curve was not, and the accounting only postponed the arithmetic.

The lesson

Capex bubbles end when unit revenue under unit cost, no matter how real the assets. Watch insider selling: it is the truest earnings report.
