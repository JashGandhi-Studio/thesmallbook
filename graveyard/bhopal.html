Why Did Bhopal Fail?

The Safety Systems Were All Switched Off

1984YEAR
TRUSTCAUSE
At least 3,800 dead within days by official count, estimates into the tens of thousands; a $470 million settlement after years of litigationTHE BURN

What happened

On the night of 2-3 December 1984, water entered a methyl isocyanate tank at Union Carbide's pesticide plant in Bhopal. The refrigeration was shut down, the scrubber was down for maintenance, the flare tower was out of order, the water curtains too short - every protection was off or undersized on the one night it mattered. The gas cloud killed thousands in their sleep and injured hundreds of thousands. The site remains contaminated; the litigation has never truly ended.

The fatal mistake

Cost-cutting that treated safety systems as optional overheads - staff reductions, deferred maintenance and standby equipment switched off, in a plant adjacent to dense housing.

The lesson

Safety is a cost only on the day nothing goes wrong. The measure of industrial trust is what a plant does when the margin is thin and the checklist says stop.
