Why Did Peloton Interactive Fail?

Built Factories for a Pandemic That Ended in Twelve Months

2022YEAR
☠️ STARTUPCAUSE
💸 Market cap plunged from $50B to under $2B; founder oustedTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

Peloton was the undisputed corporate king of 2020 lockdowns: luxury $2,000 stationary exercise bikes with high-energy live instructors streaming into living rooms while gyms remained closed. Its stock skyrocketed to a $50 billion market cap. Convinced the world had permanently changed, CEO John Foley committed billions to building a massive 1-million-square-foot factory in Ohio (the Peloton Output Park), acquired commercial equipment maker Precor for $420 million, and stockpiled massive component inventories. When gyms reopened in 2021, bike sales crashed. Warehouses filled with unsold inventory, the factory was canceled before completion, Foley resigned, and Peloton struggled with cash burn.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Committing billions in permanent fixed manufacturing capex on the assumption that temporary emergency lockdown demand would continue forever.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

Never build permanent physical factories to meet temporary demand spikes. Flexible contract capacity is insurance against the day normal consumer habits return.
