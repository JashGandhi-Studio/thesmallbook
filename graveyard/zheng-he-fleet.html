Why Did China's Treasure Fleet Fail?

The Navy That Ruled the Oceans, Burned by Its Own Court

1433YEAR
HISTORYCAUSE
The world's greatest fleet - hundreds of ships, 27,000-man expeditions - dismantled and left to rot within a generationTHE BURN

What happened

Between 1405 and 1433 Zheng He's treasure fleets reached East Africa decades before Portugal rounded the Cape - wooden giants with watertight compartments and compass navigation, projecting Chinese power across the Indian Ocean. Then the Confucian court, viewing trade and eunuch admirals as corrupting, cut the funding. Ocean-going construction was banned; by century's end shipwrights who could build such vessels no longer existed. A century later small European ships were carving up the trade routes China abandoned.

The fatal mistake

Strategic retreat as faction politics - the fleet was dismantled to win a court argument, and the capability, once gone, proved unrecoverable.

The lesson

Capabilities decay faster than ambitions. Strategic assets die by budget line, not by defeat - and rebuilding them costs tenfold what maintaining them did.
