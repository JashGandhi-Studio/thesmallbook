Why Did WeWork India Fail?

🏢 The Office Empire That Proved Even a Great Market Can't Save Bad Math · 2023 · 💸 Global parent WeWork bankrupt ($47B → $44M); India entity fought to survive

📜 What Happened

WeWork's India arm rode the co-working wave hard — massive offices in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Gurugram, signing long-term leases with landlords while renting desks month-to-month to startups. The model looked genius in a boom: occupancy was high, the brand was cool, and corporates loved it. Then the pandemic emptied the offices, and the mismatch revealed itself: WeWork owed landlords for 10-15 year leases while its tenants could cancel monthly. The global parent filed for bankruptcy in 2023 ($47B valuation to $44M), and the India entity — though operationally better — spent years restructuring leases and repricing. India's hottest office market couldn't save a model built on borrowed time.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Signing decade-long fixed costs while selling month-to-month revenue — the ultimate asset-liability mismatch, disguised as 'asset-light real estate'.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

If your costs are locked for 10 years and your revenue can walk out in 30 days, you are not a business — you are a bet on optimism. Match the length of your commitments to the length of your customers' promises.
