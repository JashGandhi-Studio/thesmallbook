Why Did Lilium Jet Fail?

$1.5B Burned on 36 Electric Jet Fans

2024YEAR
☠️ STARTUPCAUSE
💸 $1.5B+ invested; German insolvency filingTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

German startup Lilium promised regional air mobility with a sleek electric vertical takeoff jet using 36 small ducted electric fans embedded in tilting wings. It raised over $1.5 billion from Tencent, Baillie Gifford, and a Nasdaq SPAC, promising 175-mph intercity flights by 2025. But ducted electric fans require immense battery energy density during vertical hovering. As aerospace certification deadlines slipped, cash burn topped $200 million a year. When the German federal government rejected a 50 million euro loan guarantee in October 2024, Lilium's core subsidiaries filed for insolvency.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Choosing an aerodynamically inefficient hover architecture requiring battery energy densities that do not yet exist.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

Physics does not care about venture valuations. If your product architecture requires battery energy densities that violate current chemistry, you are burning capital on hope.
