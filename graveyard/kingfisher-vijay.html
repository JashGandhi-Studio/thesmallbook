Why Did Compaq & DEC Fail?

Digital's Founder: 'No Reason Anyone Would Want a Computer at Home'

💸 The minicomputer empireTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

Ken Olsen built DEC into the world's #2 computer company — the minicomputer king. In 1977 he declared there was 'no reason for any individual to have a computer in his home.' DEC dismissed PCs as toys for a decade, launched three incompatible half-hearted PCs at once in 1983 (all flopped), and kept perfecting minicomputers as the world moved to desktops. The proud engineering giant sold itself to PC-maker Compaq in 1998 — bought by the very category it had laughed at.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Graded the disruptive toy by the standards of the serious machine — and let identity ('we make real computers') veto the market's verdict.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

The toy that hooks amateurs today eats the professional market tomorrow. When you catch yourself saying 'that's not a real X' — that's the disruption announcing itself.
