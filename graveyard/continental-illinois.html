Why Did Continental Illinois Fail?

Too big to fail learns its name

1984YEAR
MONEYCAUSE
Rescued and effectively nationalised in 1984THE BURN

What happened

Continental Illinois was a major American bank that lent heavily into energy and bought troubled loans from Penn Square. It looked like scale and sophistication from Chicago. Bad energy loans and a modern electronic bank run forced an FDIC rescue in 1984 so large it popularised the idea of too big to fail. Shareholders and management were swept aside while the bank survived under control.

The fatal mistake

Mistaking size for safety while asset quality rotted.

The lesson

A rescue can save the system and still bury the institution that needed it.
