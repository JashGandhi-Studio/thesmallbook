Why Did Boeing's Culture Trade Fail?

🛫 When the Accountants Bought the Engineers · 2019 · 💸 $20B+, 346 lives, a century of trust

📜 What Happened

For 80 years Boeing was an engineers' church — 'if it's not Boeing, I'm not going.' Then the 1997 McDonnell Douglas merger put finance culture in charge: headquarters moved away from the factories, stock buybacks outranked new aircraft, and outsourcing chased quarterly margins. The 737 MAX was the endpoint — an old airframe stretched one more time to avoid a costly new design, with software (MCAS) papering over the physics and training costs minimized as a selling point. Two crashes, 346 deaths, a 20-month grounding.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Letting the scorekeepers replace the builders — every individual decision saved money, and the sum of them broke the thing that made the money possible.

Culture isn't a poster; it's who wins arguments. The day your cost-cutters can overrule your craftsmen on safety-critical calls, the countdown starts — you just don't know the number.
