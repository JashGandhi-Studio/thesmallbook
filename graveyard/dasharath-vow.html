Why Did Dasharatha Fail?

The King Destroyed by His Own Promise

~5000 BCE (epic)YEAR
☠️ EGOCAUSE
💸 His son exiled, his kingdom grieving, his own death from a broken heartTHE BURN

📜 What Happened

Dasharatha was a great king of Ayodhya who loved his son Rama more than his own life. But he had made a promise decades earlier: to his queen Kaikeyi, for saving his life in battle, he had granted her two boons to be claimed whenever she chose. She chose the worst moment — on Rama's coronation eve, she demanded Rama's exile and her son Bharata's coronation. Dasharatha had the power, the army, the love of his people, and every moral right to refuse. He had one thing he valued more: his word. He kept the vow, exiled his beloved son, and died of grief within days — a kingdom intact, a father shattered, an heir exiled by the purity of a promise.

☠️ The Fatal Mistake

Honouring a promise made in haste above the lives and futures it would destroy — the vow outlived its purpose.

🧠 The Lesson (Free for You)

A promise is not a suicide pact. The mature person knows that commitments made in one context can become prisons in another — and that the highest duty is sometimes to break an oath, pay the price, and save the people it would destroy. Dasharatha's tragedy is not that he kept his word; it is that he never asked whether the word was worth more than the son.
